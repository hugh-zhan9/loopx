# Questions for another owner

Use when the current user cannot settle a material question because another
stakeholder owns the decision or holds unavailable facts. First check available
source documents and tools; a questionnaire is not a substitute for investigation.

Use known context about the recipient and the required outcome. Ask the current
user only for missing handoff details they can supply: who can answer, and what
decision the answers must support. Do not ask them to invent the recipient's
policy or repeat questions they have already said they cannot answer.

Prepare a short, forwardable set of questions in the conversation or existing
clarification record. Write a separate questionnaire only when requested or when
an asynchronous handoff needs a file; do not send it without authorization. Include:

- enough context for someone outside the conversation to answer;
- the affected decision and source conflict, if any;
- independent questions, most consequential first, each with space for an answer;
- the specific behavior blocked by an unanswered question.

Keep a recommendation visibly separate from the recipient's answer. Permit
partial answers and “unknown”; do not invent a deadline, approval or commitment.
Do not require answers to dependent details before their prerequisites are known.

Keep unanswered requirements pending. When answers arrive, check the respondent's
authority for the decision and reconcile conflicts with existing accepted rules.
Update the owning requirements with confirmed decisions and acceptance scenarios;
the questionnaire does not become a second specification. Report the blocked
behavior without declaring unrelated requirements unresolved.
